# SAFE-SOP — General Safety Standard Operating Procedure for Machine Maintenance

Document ID: SAFE-SOP · Revision 6 · Applies to: all maintenance on CNC milling cells
Owner: EHS (Environment, Health & Safety) · Mandatory for all technicians

## 1. Principles

1.1 No production target justifies unsafe work. Any technician may stop work they believe is unsafe.

1.2 Every maintenance task that goes inside the machine guarding needs lockout/tagout (Section 3).

## 2. Personal protective equipment (PPE)

2.1 Minimum PPE inside the cell: safety glasses (EN 166), safety shoes (S3), and cut-resistant gloves (EN 388 level C or higher) when handling tools.

2.2 Wear chemical-resistant nitrile gloves when handling coolant concentrate. Coolant can cause dermatitis.

2.3 Never wear gloves near a rotating spindle. They can be caught and drag the hand in.

## 3. Lockout/Tagout (LOTO)

3.1 LOTO isolates every energy source: electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic and stored mechanical energy.

3.2 **LOTO procedure:**
1. Tell the operator and the shift lead.
2. Stop the machine with the normal stop, then wait for the spindle to stop completely (at least 10 seconds).
3. Switch the main disconnect to OFF and fit your personal lock and tag.
4. Close the pneumatic supply valve and bleed off residual pressure.
5. **Verify zero energy:** press cycle start and check that nothing moves. Check that the pressure gauges read 0 bar.
6. Only then open the guarding.

3.3 Only the person who fitted a lock may remove it. At shift handover, the incoming technician fits their lock before the outgoing technician removes theirs.

## 4. Hazards specific to failure modes

4.1 **Hot coolant and components:** after a heat dissipation event (HDF-MAN), coolant and spindle surfaces can go above 60 °C. Let the cell cool for 10 minutes before touching anything.

4.2 **Tool fragments:** after an overstrain or tool-wear break (OSF-MAN, TWF-MAN), fragments can be sharp and scattered. Stand clear of the window line until the spindle stops, and remove fragments with pliers, not bare hands.

4.3 **Electrical:** drive cabinets hold dangerous voltage for up to 5 minutes after isolation (capacitors). Only qualified electricians may open drive cabinets (see Section 5).

## 5. Qualified-person tasks

5.1 These tasks need a qualified electrician: any work inside the drive cabinet, motor insulation tests, and replacing drive power components.

5.2 These tasks need a spindle technician: bearing replacement, and drawbar or spindle disassembly.

## 6. After maintenance

6.1 Remove tools and rags from the work envelope, refit all guarding, and check that the interlocks work.

6.2 Remove LOTO in the reverse order of Section 3.2. Tell the operator before restarting.

6.3 Record the work in the maintenance log.
